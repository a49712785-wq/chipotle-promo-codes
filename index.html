Before You Default to Base Points: Check These First

Base Rewards points (10 per $1) are the baseline Rewards earning rate, not an instant discount at checkout, they're credit toward a future free entree. Three other current mechanisms can beat that baseline in immediate value on a given order, all of them personalized or rotating, so this page can tell you they exist but not what's currently in yours:

  • Your Offers tab. Chipotle sends account-specific offers that don't appear in any public list.
  • This Monday's Rewards Exchange promotion. Chipotle features a new weekly promotion in the Rewards Exchange every Monday, check what's currently live before assuming base points are your only option.
  • Any active Side Quest. Summer of Extras includes Side Quests defined per participant in-app, these aren't publicly documented and can offer bonus points beyond the base rate.

If none of those apply to your order, base points are your fallback, not your first answer, and they're a long-term earning rate rather than a same-day discount.

It does not apply to a regular individual bowl or burrito.

Chipotle Rewards: The Actual Numbers

You earn 10 points per $1 spent (tax and tip excluded) as the baseline Rewards earning rate, and 1,625 points redeems a free entree, a rate confirmed on Chipotle's own rewards page. A $59-70 Build-Your-Own Chipotle order earns 590-700 points at once, roughly 36-43% of the way to a free entree in one purchase. A typical $20 individual order earns 200 points, about 12% of the way there. This is future redemption value, not an immediate percentage off your bill, the discount only materializes once you actually redeem the points. Points stay active as long as you make one qualifying purchase a year, and they apply to every order, not one specific product the way TRYBYOC does.

Summer of Extras: Planned Purchases vs. Chasing the Reward

[Status: Seasonal Program — this year's dates confirmed above (June 1-August 31, 2026); Chipotle typically runs this each summer]

Reaching Milestone 7 (7 qualifying entree purchases within the same calendar month, 2 per day maximum) unlocks your choice of a free entree or 1,625 points. Both options are worth roughly the same, one entree's value either way, the difference is timing: the entree reward expires 7 days after you choose it, while points sit in your balance with no expiration pressure as long as you keep making qualifying purchases.

The decision depends entirely on whether the remaining purchases are ones you'd make anyway.

If yes: finishing the streak is close to pure upside, you're getting a bonus reward on spending you were already going to do.

If no, meaning you'd have to buy extra meals specifically to hit the milestone: do the math first. Two unplanned qualifying purchases at a typical $10-13 each is $20-26 in spending to unlock a reward worth roughly $10-13. That's spending close to double the reward's value just to chase it, worth it only if you'd genuinely enjoy those extra meals anyway or the gamification itself appeals to you, not as a pure savings play.

Two other variables that change the answer: when you opted in (per Chipotle's official Summer of Extras terms, if you opted in before June 30, 2026 at 11:59pm PT, purchases from the start of the promotion period count; if after, only purchases from your opt-in time forward count, nothing retroactive), and what month it is (streaks reset at the start of each calendar month, so June and July progress didn't carry into August). The program itself runs June 1 through August 31, 2026.

TAGTEAM is a Chipotle x Riot Games crossover, entering the code at digital checkout when you buy a regular-priced entree (no discount required or applied) unlocks a code redeemable at shop.riotgames.com/redeem for the Chipotle Fresh Fit Bundle, a cosmetic backpack and bucket hat for your avatar in the game 2XKO. The distinction matters: TRYBYOC and Rewards points reduce or credit your actual spending, that's a price reduction. TAGTEAM gives you a free in-game cosmetic item on top of a purchase you're already making at full price, that's a bonus, not a discount, and it shouldn't be weighed against TRYBYOC or Rewards as if it were an equivalent saving.

Worth using only if you were already planning to order a regular-priced entree through chipotle.com or the app regardless, in which case this is a genuine zero-additional-cost bonus. It's not worth buying anything extra to trigger it, and it doesn't stack with or replace TRYBYOC, Rewards points, or Summer of Extras progress, those still work the same way on this order if they otherwise apply.

Direct Ordering vs. DoorDash, Uber Eats, or Grubhub

Ordering through chipotle.com or the Chipotle app keeps you eligible for TRYBYOC, Summer of Extras progress, and Rewards points on the full order. Chipotle's own delivery is available too, typically at a higher menu price than pickup, but it's still their direct channel and still earns Rewards.

Order the same thing through a third-party platform, and none of that applies, Chipotle doesn't track those orders for Rewards at all. A $59 group order that would've earned 590 points earns zero through a delivery app, on top of whatever fee the platform itself charges.

This is a real comparison, not an automatic answer: if a delivery app's own current promotion is large enough to outweigh the points and program eligibility you'd give up, it can still be the better choice for that specific order. When no platform-specific deal is running, ordering direct usually comes out ahead, specifically because of what it preserves (points, program eligibility), not because delivery apps are inherently worse.

Is a Code You Found Elsewhere Actually Real?

Beyond the offers above, other Chipotle codes do circulate, but the ones that check out are short-lived, typically active for just 2 to 5 days around a specific event like National Avocado Day or National Quesadilla Day. Before trying one:

  • Does it read like a dated campaign, not a random string? Some legitimate event codes use recognizable campaign or event-related formats, such as a food abbreviation plus a year (the current TRYBYOC and TAGTEAM codes both follow this pattern), though format alone doesn't confirm authenticity, plenty of expired or fabricated codes look the same way.
  • Does it show up on more than one source with matching terms? A real code comes with full restrictions attached, matching across sources.
  • Does it require a Rewards account? Many legitimate Chipotle offers require a Rewards account, though not universally, so this is a signal to weigh, not a pass/fail test on its own.

In the sources checked for this page, sites listing a large number of "active" codes at once tended to include several that were expired or didn't match any verifiable pattern, that's a pattern observed in this research, not a claim about every coupon site. TRYBYOC's own history is a useful example: an expired 2025 version (different dates, different redemption cap) is still shown as current on some sites, which is exactly the kind of entry worth double-checking against chipotle.com/byoc-legal directly.

If Something Doesn't Apply at Checkout

  • Not logged into Rewards. True for nearly every offer on this page.
  • Trying to combine offers. TRYBYOC allows one discount per transaction and can't be combined with other coupons or promotions.
  • A window closed or a cap was hit. TRYBYOC's 100,000-redemption cap, Chipotle IQ's hourly prize pool, or a dated event code's few-day window.
  • Wrong channel. TRYBYOC and Rewards require chipotle.com or the app, not in-restaurant, not catering, not third-party delivery, the same restriction that costs you Rewards points on a delivery app order.

Bottom Line

  • Solo order: check your Offers tab, this week's Rewards Exchange promotion, and any active Side Quest before defaulting to baseline Rewards points.
  • Group of 4-6: compare TRYBYOC on a Build-Your-Own Chipotle order against ordering individual meals for your group's size.
  • Already close to Summer of Extras Milestone 7: finish the streak only if the remaining purchases are ones you'd make anyway.
  • Considering third-party delivery: weigh the platform's actual current promotion against the Rewards points and program eligibility you'd give up by not ordering direct.
  • Found a random coupon code: verify it against chipotle.com before relying on it at checkout.
  • Free promotional game or in-game bonus (Chipotle IQ, TAGTEAM): treat it as conditional upside on an order you're already making, not a guaranteed saving worth planning around.
